# demand-splitting

The pre-funnel ends here: a diagnosis becomes the demands the funnel will carry.
The cut is the whole value of the step. A diagnosis handed over whole is not a
demand, it is a wish; five demands carved out of one finding are five ways to
argue about the same thing.

## One demand per intervention

A demand is something a team could pick up and finish. If two parts of a proposed
change could ship in different quarters, they are two demands. If neither is worth
anything without the other, they are one.

The test is not size, it is INDEPENDENT VALUE: after this ships and nothing else
does, is somebody better off? If the honest answer is no, it is not its own demand.

## Enablers before optimisations

Where the diagnosis records a knock-out — no timestamps, no interface access, no
accountable owner — the enabler is the ONLY admissible demand until it is cleared.
An optimisation demand raised on an unmeasurable process is a promise nobody can
check, and it will be scored against evidence that does not exist.

Say this explicitly when you cut the list short: "the remaining findings cannot be
turned into demands until X is obtainable" is a finding in its own right, and a
more useful one than four demands that cannot be evaluated.

## Smallest valuable cut first

Order the demands so the first one is the smallest that delivers value alone. This
is not a courtesy to the funnel — it is what makes the next iteration possible at
all, because the second demand is written with evidence the first one produced.

## Every demand carries its basis

Each demand names the part of the diagnosis it comes from — the dimension, the
knock-out, the section — so a reviewer can go back and check. A demand whose basis
is "the analysis" is a demand nobody can challenge, and one nobody can challenge
is one nobody can improve. See `evidence-standards`.

## What you do not do

Do not invent findings the diagnosis does not contain. Do not merge unrelated
frictions into a single large programme because it looks more significant. Do not
propose a technology when the diagnosis points at the process design — the shape
of the intervention comes from the branch the diagnosis chose, not from what is
currently fashionable.
