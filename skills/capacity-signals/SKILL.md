# capacity-signals

Capacity is the constraint the hub-and-spoke model actually runs into. This skill
reads the load signals the portal already holds and turns them into support, never
into assessment.

## The signals available, and what each is worth

- **Engagements owned** — the strongest signal. Owning a process diagnosis means
  decisions land on this person.
- **Engagements championed** — carrying the work locally. Costs time steadily
  rather than in spikes.
- **Demands raised** — evidence of engagement with the portal, and a rough proxy
  for how much this person is trying to change. A high count with no engagements
  usually means someone who keeps asking and is not being served.
- **Stated capacity** — what they said they have. Where it conflicts with the
  counts above, the conflict IS the finding, and it is the most actionable one on
  the page: somebody with "half a day a week" owning four engagements is a
  commitment the hub made and has not honoured.

## Turning a signal into support

For each node, ask what would help, in this order:

1. **Relieve** — is something they carry better carried by someone else, or by
   the hub temporarily?
2. **Second person** — would a co-champion at the same site make this durable
   rather than heroic?
3. **Stop asking** — is the honest answer that this person is full, and the next
   engagement in their area must wait or go elsewhere? Saying so is a service to
   them and to whoever would otherwise wait for a reply that never comes.

## The frame

Nothing here is a performance reading. Load is what the ORGANISATION has asked of
a person; it says nothing about how well they work. Never order people by load,
never characterise a low count as disengagement — a low count with an uncovered
domain around it means nobody was asked, not that nobody cared.
