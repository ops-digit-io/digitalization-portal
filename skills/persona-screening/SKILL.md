# persona-screening

Turn a requestor's own demands into an understanding of their **work**, so the Digital
Unit can serve them better. This is a "know your requestor" reading — like customer
understanding, pointed inward at the people who raise demand — not a judgement of the
person.

## Read four things

1. **Role & domain focus.** Where their demands cluster — domains, plants, lanes. This
   infers the part of the business they operate in and the systems they touch. Report
   as shares ("60% quality, 40% maintenance"), not a label you pin on them.
2. **Jobs & daily workflows.** The recurring themes across their problem / current-pain
   / desired-outcome / affected-process text, and the concrete processes they name.
   These describe what their day actually involves — the jobs they keep trying to get
   done. Surface the recurring nouns and the named processes, not one-off words.
3. **Solution-archetype needs** (see `usecase-archetypes`). Which solution shapes they
   tend to reach for — analytics, automation, prediction, GenAI, self-service… This
   tells you how to help: someone who keeps asking for dashboards may next need the
   prediction step; someone asking for automation may need process rework first.
4. **Digitalization maturity — described, not scored.** How they frame demands:
   do they quantify a baseline, name the process and owner, state frequency/scale, and
   do their demands progress past intake? Report each as a plain count
   ("names the process in 4 of 6 demands"). This calibrates how much analysis support
   they need — it is NOT a rating of the person.

## Cohort patterns

Aggregate across requestor groups: for a domain/lane/plant cohort, which archetypes and
themes recur across its requestors. Only report a cohort with **at least two distinct
requestors**, so an aggregate can never point at one person. Cohorts reveal, e.g.,
"maintenance requestors mostly need prediction, but few quantify a baseline" — a
signal for where to invest enablement.

## What good looks like

- The profile reads like **help for the requestor**: "here's what they keep working on,
  here's the support that would serve them next."
- Themes are the recurring, meaningful terms — not stopwords, not one-offs.
- Maturity is a set of facts a requestor would recognise and not resent.
- Cohort insight is genuinely aggregate and actionable for enablement.

## Guardrails (hard)

- **Descriptive, never evaluative.** No score, grade, or rating of a person.
- **Never rank or compare individuals.** No leaderboards, no "most active requestor".
  The directory is alphabetical by design.
- **Aggregate cohorts only** (≥2 requestors). Never let a cohort resolve to one person.
- **Serve, don't surveil.** The requestor is the beneficiary and can see their own
  profile. Never produce anything for a performance or HR assessment.
- If asked to rank/score/compare people, refuse and restate this frame.
