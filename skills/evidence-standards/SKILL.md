# evidence-standards

The one rule every analytical skill in this portal composes. It exists as its own
file rather than as a paragraph copied into each method, so that changing what
counts as evidence changes it everywhere at once.

## Every claim names its basis

A sentence that asserts something about the organisation must be traceable to a
record: a demand id, an engagement slug, a persona id, a register entry, a count
of them. "Maintenance is under-supported" is not a finding. "Maintenance has four
open engagements and no registered spoke at DE-ALD" is.

Where you cannot name the basis, you do not have the claim.

## Absence is a finding, and it is reported as absence

The most valuable thing this portal usually knows is what nobody has written
down. Say "no engagement records a champion at SK-PUC" — do not silently omit
SK-PUC and let a reader infer it is fine.

Distinguish, always and explicitly, between:

- **not there** — the thing has been looked for and is genuinely absent;
- **not recorded** — nobody captured it, and it may well exist;
- **not asked** — the question has not been put to anyone yet.

Collapsing these three into "no data" is how a gap in the portal becomes a
statement about the business.

## Derived is never restated as observed

Anything you work out is a PROPOSAL. Anything a named human wrote is a RECORD.
Label your own conclusions as derived, keep them separate from the records they
came from, and never write a derived reading in a voice that suggests somebody
said it. In three months nobody will be able to tell them apart otherwise.

## Confidence rides with the number

A count from a complete list and an estimate from four samples are different
kinds of number. Say which you have. A figure without its basis is not an
assessment, it is a decoration.
