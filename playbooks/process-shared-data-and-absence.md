# Shared — data demand, and absence

Two question families that belong to every section and every advisory pass. Raise them inside
whatever is already being discussed — a step, a figure, a report, a tool — never as a block of their
own. Two questions in context cost nothing; an appended questionnaire costs the room's patience.

## 1. Data demand and reliability

The sections ask what data exists. Also ask the reverse: **which data would this process actually
need?** A named gap between what the work demands and what is held is a finding on its own.

**When:** whenever a figure, field, list or report is named, by anyone.

**Ask, in this order:**

1. "What decision rests on this figure?" No nameable decision makes it an absence candidate, part 2.
2. "How wrong could it be before you would decide differently?" That is the accuracy the process
   **demands**, and it comes out as a tolerance: ±2 days, to the pallet, to the cent.
3. "And how wrong is it actually — how do you know that?" That is the accuracy **held**.
4. "Who other than you holds this figure, or could hold it?"

**Real answer versus reflex.** A demanded accuracy is real when it is a tolerance in units tied to
that decision. "It has to be exact", "100 %", "as accurate as possible" are reflexes; push
once — "if it were one day out, what would you do differently?" An accuracy held is real when it
comes with a mechanism: a check somebody runs, a correction somebody made, a rework case. "It is
reliable" is not an answer; "we cross-checked against the delivery notes last month and three were
wrong" is.

**The gap is the signal, both ways.** Demanded above held means decisions are being taken on a number
that cannot carry them. Held far above demanded means effort is spent on precision nobody uses —
cheap to hand back, and one of the fastest increments available.

**Who else could hold it.** This is what turns the diagnosis of one process into a cross-departmental
one, so do not let it pass as a department name. Push for a person or a system, and for how their
version differs from this one. Two areas maintaining the same figure separately is either a
duplication to remove or a disagreement nobody has noticed. Inbound direction only — who would
*benefit from receiving* information out of this process is the knowledge section's job.

## 2. Absence

**Removing something is a first-class outcome, not the leftover when nothing else fits** — and the
cheapest increment there is: nothing to build, nothing to migrate, nothing to train. Probe for it
actively.

**When:** at every step, artefact, field, report, approval, meeting and copy the session names —
especially when it looks obviously necessary.

**Ask:** "If this did not exist at all for the next ten runs, what would break, and who would notice
first, by name?"

**Real answer versus reflex.** The reflexes are "we need it for the audit", "for traceability", "the
customer requires it", "everything would stop". Each gets one follow-up: which rule, who owns it,
can you point at it? A requirement nobody can point to is not a requirement. A real answer names a
person, a rule you could open, or a case where the thing was missing or late and something followed.

**Name the cost of killing.** An absence is a change like any other and it has a price: informal
consumers nobody listed, evidence that goes with it — a killed step also kills its timestamps, so
say so — and the rebuild if it turns out to have been needed. A removal without a named cost is a
guess, and the first person it surprises will reverse it.

**Never write "can be dropped."** Write what was checked, what was not, and who could not be named —
the list that officially nobody needs is usually feeding a neighbour informally.
